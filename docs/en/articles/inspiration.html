Our project story

A familiar camera, and the idea behind a Thai menu

Updated 5 November 2026 · Project notes

This project began with a simple thought: there is still plenty to learn from an older camera, and its tools could be easier for Thai-speaking photographers to explore.

Building on work we value

Magic Lantern and its community have created tools that bring new capabilities to many cameras. Their work inspired us to learn more and contribute in a way that felt close to home: through the language we use every day.

We are starting with the Canon EOS 600D running Canon firmware 1.0.2. Our work is an independent fork, developed separately from the upstream project. We want to make menu names and explanations more approachable for Thai-speaking users while preserving the original contributors’ credits.

A little easier to read and explore

Translation is one part of the plan. We also want larger menu text and a layout that is comfortable to read on a small camera screen. That means taking time to check Thai vowels and tone marks, and making sure descriptions fit the available space.

Some names, such as Focus Peaking and Histogram, are already familiar to many photographers. We want Thai explanations to make their meaning clearer while keeping the connection to the English terms, so readers can continue learning from other guides.

An invitation to discover open source

We hope this small project encourages more people in Thailand to explore software for photography. That might begin with wondering how a menu works, suggesting a clearer translation, or trying to read and change a little code.

Open source allows people to study and build on software under its license terms. For this project, that means sharing source, recording our changes and crediting the work we use. Being clear about where AI has helped is also part of explaining our process openly.

Small contributions are welcome

You do not need to know how to code to join in. If a phrase is confusing, a menu name could be clearer, or there is a topic you would like us to explain, you can share an idea through GitHub. If you have only read the material and have not tried it on a camera, saying so helps us understand your feedback.

Thank you to the Magic Lantern community, the contributors behind its dependencies, and everyone who shares what they know. We hope a Thai menu can be the start of many more things to learn and share.
